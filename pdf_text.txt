ALPHA ADVENTIST PRE & PRIMARY SCHOOL 
OFFICIAL WEBSITE MASTER TEMPLATE 
 
The proposed Alpha Adventist Pre & Primary School official website should be developed as a 
modern, secure and comprehensive digital platform that represents the school's educational mission, 
Christian identity, academic programmes, technology development, talents, character formation and 
future direction. The central identity of the website should remain: 
 
Education • Faith • Technology • Talent • Character • Future 
An important institutional point must be clearly represented throughout the website: Alpha Adventist 
Pre & Primary School is a Seventh-day Adventist educational institution operating within the 
Western Tanzania Conference of the Seventh-day Adventist Church, Kigoma, Tanzania. This 
relationship is supported by the school's documentation, which identifies the institution under the 
Western Tanzania Conference of SDA and records formal school-board governance and activities. The 
website should therefore not present Alpha as an independent generic private school; its Seventh-day 
Adventist educational identity and relationship with the Western Tanzania Conference should be visible, 
appropriately worded and consistently represented. 
 
The website should communicate the philosophy of holistic Adventist education, where academic 
development is integrated with spiritual, physical, social, moral and practical development. Alpha's own 
reporting reflects this broader approach through spiritual programmes, academic development, staff 
development and future institutional plans. Consequently, Faith and Character should not appear 
merely as marketing slogans. They should be integrated into the About, School Life, Spiritual Life, 
Academics and Student Development sections of the website.The platform should serve five principal 
audiences: pupils, parents and guardians, teachers, school workers, and members of the community 
who may participate in future computer training. Children need a colourful, simple and engaging 
experience; parents need reliable information and straightforward communication; teachers and workers 
need professional resources and training opportunities; and the wider community should be able to 
discover future educational and computer-training programmes. The website therefore needs different 
user journeys while maintaining one consistent Alpha identity. 
 
For parents and guardians, the website should establish trust immediately. They should easily find 
information concerning academics, admissions, boarding and day arrangements, school programmes, 
calendars, examinations, school rules, contacts, news and achievements. The available school registration 
information records learners across KG I through Standard VII, including both boarding and day 
pupils, which supports having dedicated sections for the different levels and school arrangements.  
For children, the website should be friendly rather than institutional or text-heavy. A dedicated Alpha 
Kids Zone can provide computer lessons, typing activities, mathematics and science exercises, reading 
resources, quizzes, creative activities and internet-safety education. Large photographs, icons, simple 
language and age-appropriate interactive content should be used while avoiding excessive animation, 
advertising and unsafe external links. 
 
Computer and Digital Learning should become one of Alpha's major distinguishing programmes. 
Computer education should support Alpha pupils from basic computer operation and typing through 
Microsoft Office, internet skills, digital safety, file management, creative technology and introductory 
coding. The programme can also provide ICT development for teachers and school workers, improving 
digital teaching, administration, records management, communication and cybersecurity awareness. 
The same digital-learning programme can progressively develop into an Alpha Computer Training 
Centre. Training for outsiders should initially be presented as ―Coming Soon‖ until formally launched. 
Future participants could include parents, youth, school leavers, job seekers, businesspeople, teachers 
from other schools, workers and other community members. Proposed courses could include Computer 
Fundamentals, Microsoft Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Internet & Email, Digital Office Skills, Cyber Safety 
and introductory coding. Successful participants could receive an authorised Certificate of Completion,

supported later by secure online certificate verification. The website should also showcase talent 
development and school life. Dedicated content should cover music, choir, sports, art, drama, public 
speaking, leadership, ICT activities, creativity, educational trips, worship and community service. This is 
particularly important because the school's identity should demonstrate that Alpha develops the whole

The homepage should therefore immediately present Alpha's pupils, teachers, campus, computer

should quickly understand: this is a Seventh-day Adventist school; it provides serious education; it 
develops character; it embraces technology; it develops children's talents; and parents can trust 
and communicate with the institution. The website should also become Alpha's official 
communication centre. News, announcements, school calendars, examinations, graduation, academic 
achievements, ICT programmers, sports and spiritual events should be published through the website. 
Facebook, Instagram and YouTube should complement rather than replace it. Social-media posts should 
increasingly direct parents and community members back to the official Alpha website for complete and 
authoritative information. 
 
From a technical perspective, the website should be mobile-first, responsive, fast, accessible, search-
engine optimised and secure. It should contain a content management system allowing authorised staff 
to manage news, events, galleries, courses, admissions, contacts and announcements. Security controls 
should include HTTPS, secure administrator authentication, role-based permissions, regular backups, 
form protection and controlled access to sensitive information.Privacy and child safeguarding must be 
incorporated into the design from the beginning. Approved school information, programmes, statistics, 
events and authorised media can be public, while individual pupil results, private parent information, staff 
records, internal board documents, safeguarding information and other sensitive records should remain 
protected. This separation is particularly important for a school website involving children's information. 
Development should be progressive. Phase One should establish the official public website, academics, 
Adventist identity, admissions, computer learning, school life, news, gallery and contact services. Phase 
Two can introduce online admissions, computer-course registration, events and certificate management. 
Phase Three can introduce secure Parent, Student and Teacher portals for results, attendance, learning 
materials, notices and communication. Later phases could support e-learning, online payments, certificate 
verification, digital libraries and other integrated educational services.

the Western Tanzania Conference – Kigoma, committed to holistic Christian education that 
develops learners academically, spiritually, socially, creatively and technologically. Through quality 
education, faith, discipline, character development, talent discovery and digital skills, Alpha 
prepares children to succeed in school, in society and in the future. The website should ultimately 
make Alpha recognisable as a school of Education, a school of Faith, a school of Technology, a school 
of Talent, a school of Character and a school preparing children for the Future.

Computer Learning for Alpha Students 
Learn • Practise • Explore • Create 
At Alpha Adventist Pre & Primary School, computer education is designed to help pupils 
develop practical digital skills gradually according to their age and learning level. 
The programme begins with foundational computer knowledge and safe use of technology, 
giving learners confidence to understand, operate and care for digital equipment while preparing 
them for more advanced ICT learning in the future. 
What Alpha Students Currently Learn 
Introduction to Computers 
Learners begin by understanding what a computer is, how computers are used and how 
technology helps people perform different tasks. 
Computer Parts and Devices 
Pupils learn to identify common computer components such as the monitor, system unit, 
keyboard, mouse, printer and speakers, and understand the basic purpose of each device. 
Input and Output Devices 
Learners are introduced to devices used to enter information into a computer, such as keyboards, 
mice, microphones and scanners, as well as output devices such as monitors, speakers, printers 
and projectors. 
Keyboard Skills and Basic Typing 
Pupils practise using letters, numbers, the Space Bar and other basic keyboard functions. Simple 
typing activities help them build confidence and accuracy when entering information. 
Mouse Skills 
Learners practise essential mouse actions including left-clicking, double-clicking, dragging 
and scrolling, enabling them to select, open, move and navigate items on the computer. 
Opening, Saving and Managing Work 
Students learn how to open the correct program, enter information, save their work using 
appropriate commands such as Ctrl + S, and develop basic awareness of files and folders. 
Starting and Shutting Down Computers 
Pupils learn the correct procedures for switching computers on and off, including checking 
equipment, waiting for the system to load, saving work, closing programs and shutting down 
correctly. 
Computer Lab Rules and Safety 
Safe and responsible use of computers is an important part of the programme. Learners are 
taught how to behave properly in the computer laboratory, handle equipment carefully and 
follow teacher instructions. 
Internet Awareness and Digital Safety 
Children are introduced to responsible online behaviour, including using approved websites, 
protecting passwords, avoiding unsafe downloads, protecting personal information and reporting 
suspicious online activities. 
Smartboard and Practical Learning 
ICT lessons include interactive smartboard activities, demonstrations, questions and supervised 
practical exercises. Pupils may also work in pairs so that they can learn through cooperation and 
practice.

53. WHY THIS WEBSITE ARCHITECTURE IS JUSTIFIED 
The architecture works because it serves four audiences simultaneously—prospective families, 
existing parents, pupils and the wider school community—without forcing them through the 
same user journey. For children, it provides visual and interactive learning. For parents, it 
prioritises admissions, academics, calendars, notices and communication. For management, it 
creates a controlled publishing platform. For ICT development, it establishes an expandable 
foundation for future digital services. Most importantly, the architecture distinguishes public 
information from private records. Results, attendance, fees and individual communications 
can later sit behind authenticated portals, while institutional information remains public. 
It also gives Alpha one authoritative digital identity. Social media becomes the distribution layer; 
the website remains the institutional reference point.
